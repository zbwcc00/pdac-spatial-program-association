# Local association of mregDC-like and T-cell/lymphoid transcriptional programs in PDAC

**Running title:** Local mregDC-like and lymphoid program association in PDAC

**Authors:** Bowen Zheng^1^, Yuqiao Sun^1^, Jinyuan Chi^1^, Hao Li^1^*

**Affiliations:** ^1^Division of Hepatobiliary Pancreatic Surgery, The Affiliated Hospital of Yanbian University, Yanji 133000, China

**Corresponding author:** Hao Li, Division of Hepatobiliary Pancreatic Surgery, The Affiliated Hospital of Yanbian University, Yanji 133000, China. Email: lih@ybu.edu.cn

## Abstract

### Background

Tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS)-associated immune signals are of interest in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), but a lymphoid transcriptional program does not establish TLS morphology. We tested whether fixed mregDC-like and operational Tfh-like programs were associated after local smoothing of spatial-transcriptomic spot scores.

### Methods

In coordinate-resolved GSE278687 (21 sections from 18 patients), fixed program scores were residualized for library size, `PTPRC`, and epithelial and stromal proxies; the mregDC-like score was additionally residualized for DC-core. We correlated focal-spot-excluded six-nearest-neighbour fields within spots above the section mean for DC-core and aggregated repeated sections to patient medians. A hierarchy-matched patient-level array-block null was the primary randomization analysis. The same pipeline was applied to 18 scoreable GSE277116 tumour packages, a neoadjuvant-treatment-context cohort without public patient identifiers, as sample-level technical replication. Graph-null, conditioning/mask, neighbourhood-size, competitive-program, scoreability, and single-cell analyses were sensitivity, descriptive, or attribution-QC analyses.

### Results

In GSE278687, the patient-level median local correlation was 0.418 (bootstrap 95% CI 0.312-0.516; 17/18 positive); the primary block null produced 0/999 at-least-as-extreme draws (two-sided add-one Monte Carlo P=0.001). Global- and mask-Moran graph sensitivities had the same direction. In GSE277116, the median across 18 packages was 0.414 (95% CI 0.287-0.470; 18/18 positive); this is sample-level technical replication, not patient-level validation. Whole-tissue, DC-core-threshold, and k=4/6/12 analyses remained positive. Broad-T adjustment retained positive medians (0.331 and 0.350), whereas non-Tfh CD4 was similarly associated (0.436 and 0.440), arguing against Tfh specificity. `CCL19` deletion attenuated the association (0.303 and 0.203). In an independent author-annotated PDAC single-cell reference, the fixed mregDC-like score was higher in activated DCs than cDC2 (23 paired patients; median difference 0.479, 95% CI 0.424-0.668; P=2.38e-6); minimum-cell analyses were exploratory. The reference had no author-defined Tfh label.

### Conclusions

Within the stated score, residualization, mask, and smoothing definitions, the datasets show a directionally concordant local transcriptional-program association. They do not identify a cellular niche, TLS morphology or maturation, cellular contact, mechanism, causality, prognosis, or treatment response.

**Keywords:** pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma; spatial transcriptomics; dendritic-cell transcriptional program; lymphoid transcriptional programs; spatial association; spatial statistics

## Introduction

PDAC contains a dense stromal compartment that shapes intratumoral architecture [1]. Single-cell studies have identified PDAC cell states that coexist with site-specific microenvironments [2]. A multimodal immune atlas also found patient-specific checkpoint-receptor expression and dysfunctional CD8+ T-cell states [3].

TLS and B-cell-rich aggregates are of interest because histologically identified B-cell/TLS features were associated with immune-checkpoint-blockade response in melanoma and renal-cell carcinoma [4]. More generally, TLS are ectopic lymphoid aggregates whose formation and contributions to intratumoral immunity remain context dependent [5]. A spatial study of renal-cell cancer showed that TLS can contain B-cell maturation states and antibody-producing plasma cells, illustrating why a transcriptomic lymphoid signal may be biologically relevant without defining morphology in a different tumour type [6]. In PDAC, IL-33-responsive ILC2 biology has been linked experimentally to TLS induction in mouse models and to putative TLS-associated cells in human samples [7]. Recent PDAC spatial studies have separately mapped neural-invasion microenvironments [8], mature TLS after neoadjuvant immunotherapy [9], and immune dysfunction in treatment-naive disease [10].

None of these studies permits a transcriptomic lymphoid score to substitute for a morphology-defined TLS. TLS are structured ectopic lymphoid aggregates and require evidence beyond expression measured in bulk tissue or spatial-capture units [11].

The mregDC framework describes a mature regulatory dendritic-cell program that includes `CCR7` and emerges after tumour-antigen uptake [12]. Pan-cancer single-cell data show that LAMP3+ conventional DC states vary with lineage and stimulus [13], and human DC phenotypes in cancer remain context dependent [14]. Tfh cells provide B-cell help in lymphoid settings [15], so an operational Tfh-like score offers a biologically motivated lymphoid comparator. A spatial mregDC and CD4+ T-helper configuration has been described after PD-1 blockade in hepatocellular carcinoma [16]. That finding does not predict a direct mregDC and Tfh relationship in PDAC, nor do these gene lists assign either cell state within a spatial-capture unit.

Spatial transcriptomics measures expression at defined tissue locations rather than assigning cell identity directly [17]. Studies of tissue architecture and integration with single-cell references must therefore account for capture-unit composition and resolution [18,19]. Non-uniform cellular density can also confound spatial heterogeneity [20]. Spatial outcomes are often autocorrelated [21,22], so unconstrained outcome permutations discard part of the observed structure. Moran spectral randomization provides one approach for generating spatially constrained surrogate outcomes [23].

We asked a narrow computational question: given fixed scores, covariates, a mask, and local smoothing, is the mregDC-like program locally associated with the operational Tfh-like program? The latter is interpreted within a broader T-cell/lymphoid context. GSE278687 provides patient-level inference, whereas GSE277116 provides sample-level technical replication. Spatially constrained nulls, non-overlapping reference programs, and gene-detectability QC define the scope of the analysis.

## Materials and methods

### Cohorts and evidentiary roles

We used GSE237846 as targeted-panel segmented-cell and marker-detectability context, not as independent patient-level confirmation [24]. GSE205354 supported descriptive same-spot covariation because its public archive lacks coordinates [10]. GSE278687 was the primary coordinate-resolved cohort (21 Visium sections from 18 patients) [8]. After auditing matrices, coordinates, barcode matching, gene coverage, and processing strata, we retained 18 complete scoreable GSE277116 tumour packages (8 FFPE and 10 frozen) for the technical-replication analysis [9]. GSE277116 lacks public patient identifiers, and the archive cannot resolve whether packages share patients; it supports sample-level technical replication only. Table 1 summarizes these cohorts and their evidentiary roles. GSE217845 was used only for supplementary marker-gated gene-detectability and gating-feasibility QC [24]. GSE202051 was an independent author-annotated PDAC single-cell reference for fixed-program attribution and specificity QC [25]. GEO is the source archive [26]. TCGA-PAAD was used only as a bulk-expression boundary analysis, not as a spatial surrogate [27].

### Frozen programs and normalized scoring

The DC-core program comprised `FCER1A`, `CLEC10A`, `CD1C`, `CST3`, `HLA-DRA`, `HLA-DPA1`, and `HLA-DPB1`. The mregDC-like score used `LAMP3`, `FSCN1`, `CCR7`, `CCL19`, `CD80`, and `CD86`; the Tfh-like score used `CD3D`, `CD3E`, `CD4`, `CXCR5`, `PDCD1`, `ICOS`, `BCL6`, `IL7R`, and `CXCL13`. These literature-informed lists [12,15] were operational scoring definitions, not unique markers of canonical cell states. We summed log1p-transformed expression across detected program genes and standardized scores within sample before residualization and local-field construction. The v2 primary pipeline fixed the programs, primary estimand, covariates, exclusions, seed, and spatial settings before the unified rerun, but was not prospectively preregistered. The rationale for subsequent v3-v7 null, scoreability, and sensitivity analyses is recorded in Supplementary Data S2. We audited per-sample gene coverage and zero-score fractions for all primary and reference programs (Supplementary Table S17).

### Unified primary spatial analysis

Within each section, we residualized mregDC-like scores by least squares against standardized log library size, `PTPRC`, epithelial proxy, stromal proxy, and DC-core. We residualized Tfh-like scores against the first four covariates. Each local field was the mean residual across the six nearest spatial neighbours, excluding the focal spot. The primary Pearson correlation used only spots with DC-core scores above the section mean. This double conditioning targets residual mregDC-like variation in DC-core-enriched tissue; it does not estimate a tumour-wide association. We collapsed GSE278687 sections to patient medians and summarized the 18 patient medians with percentile bootstrap confidence intervals. GSE277116 was summarized analogously at sample level. GSE278687 uses image-pixel coordinates and GSE277116 uses array coordinates, so their raw distances are not treated as comparable micrometre scales. Section-specific distributions are provided in Supplementary Table S27, with post-hoc k=4/6/12 results in Supplementary Table S26 and Figure S8.

### Spatially constrained null inference

For the array-block null, we permuted the Tfh-like outcome within 8 x 8 array-coordinate blocks in each section and held the mregDC-like residual field fixed. We recomputed section effects at every draw, collapsed repeated GSE278687 sections to patient medians, and recalculated the cohort median. The null therefore follows the same inferential hierarchy as the observed statistic. It is an outcome-randomization test conditional on the observed mregDC-like field, not a model of a joint bivariate spatial process.

For the graph-constrained null, we generated rank-preserving, graph-filtered Tfh-like outcome surrogates on a symmetric six-nearest-neighbour graph while holding the observed mregDC-like residual field fixed. Interpolation and bisection calibrated graph-filter strength to the observed whole-section outcome Moran's I in every section and draw, followed by the same local-field and cohort aggregation pipeline. We report Moran mismatch, null ranges, the number of at-least-as-extreme draws, and two-sided add-one Monte Carlo P values, `(1 + extreme)/(B + 1)`, as described by Phipson and Smyth [28]. Global-targeted batches used four independently seeded deterministic NumPy streams of 250 draws each (20360813, 20460813, 20560813, and 20660813). In this analysis, mask-specific Moran's I was audited but not calibrated. A separate mask-targeted sensitivity calibrated Moran's I on the induced DC-core subgraph with the same batching scheme. A one-parameter filter cannot generally match global and mask Moran's I simultaneously, so each sensitivity reports mismatch on the alternative scale. Both graph analyses are outcome-only conditional sensitivities. The patient-level block null remains the primary randomization analysis.

### Leakage-aware spatial block holdout

We held out contiguous 8 x 8 array-coordinate blocks within each section. Residualization coefficients and the DC-core threshold were fitted using non-held-out spots only. Local fields in a held-out block used only its held-out neighbours and excluded the focal spot. For each unit, we calculated the median across eligible held-out blocks; repeated GSE278687 sections were then collapsed to patient medians. Percentile bootstrap confidence intervals summarize the median unit-level holdout effect [29]. Figure 5 presents this leakage-aware holdout and leave-one-unit sensitivity. This assesses whether the direction persists when local fields cannot draw on the full section; it is not another primary null-hypothesis test. Leave-one-patient-out (GSE278687) and leave-one-sample-out (GSE277116) recalculation assessed unit influence on the locked cohort median.

### Specificity and competition analyses

We discarded the earlier non-Tfh CD4 control because it overlapped with Tfh-like genes. Broad-T, non-Tfh CD4, Treg, exhausted-CD8, conventional-DC, and macrophage reference programs were constructed without overlap with the frozen Tfh-like signature. We evaluated association after broad-T adjustment, local mregDC-like association with each reference program, a local-field joint model including Tfh-like and all reference programs, and sensitivity to removal of `CCL19`. Because residual spatial correlation is not modeled, joint-model coefficients and variance-inflation factors are descriptive spot-field quantities rather than spatially valid independent-effect tests. These comparisons test robustness across specified transcriptional contexts, not cell-type specificity.

### Program scoreability and supplementary single-cell QC

Post-hoc scoreability sensitivities, specified before that sensitivity rerun, retained units only when both fixed primary program scores were nonzero in at least 0.20, 0.30, 0.40 or 0.50 of tissue spots. In GSE278687, 17, 16, 16 and 16 of 18 patients were retained, with median local correlations of 0.468, 0.469, 0.469 and 0.469. In GSE277116, 16, 12, 10 and 7 of 18 packages were retained, with corresponding medians of 0.396, 0.414, 0.396 and 0.384. These estimates are descriptive robustness summaries with declining effective sample size, particularly in GSE277116; no threshold was selected as a replacement primary analysis (Supplementary Table S22). Supplementary Tables S1-S16, S20, S23-S25, S28, and S30-S33 provide the programs, unit-level results, null and holdout summaries, scoreability audits, annotation crosswalk, and single-cell attribution results; the complete table inventory and file mapping are in `supplementary_pack_manifest_v7.json`.

All mregDC-like, Tfh-like, and non-overlapping reference-program genes were represented in the GSE277116 packages, but scoreability varied by platform. Median mregDC-like and Tfh-like zero-score fractions were 0.277 and 0.140 in GSE278687 and 0.538 and 0.529 in GSE277116. Some competitive programs were especially sparse in GSE277116, including conventional DC (0.914) and Treg (0.844; Supplementary Table S17). These comparisons describe spot-level transcriptional patterns rather than cell abundance. The marker-gated GSE217845 analysis appears only in Supplementary Figure S1 as gene-detectability and gating-feasibility QC. In GSE202051, we integrity-checked the archived integrated object and retained author-provided level-1 to level-3 labels without relabelling. We scored fixed programs as the mean author-provided log-normalized expression of program genes minus an expression-bin-matched control mean (25 bins; up to 50 controls per target gene; seed 20260814); the unadjusted program mean was a scoring sensitivity. Raw-UMI detection came from `counts`, whose sparse zero pattern agreed with `X`. The unit of comparison was the within-patient median score for each author label, assessed with two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The designated primary comparison was the matched-control mregDC-like score in activated DCs versus cDC2. It was specified for this module but not prospectively preregistered. All other label comparisons, score variants, leave-one-gene-out tests, and minimum-cell-per-label sensitivities were exploratory and were adjusted within that family by Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction [30]. Median-difference 95% confidence intervals used 10,000 bootstrap resamples. This module assesses program attribution and non-specificity boundaries, not the spatial association, cell-cell contact or mechanism.

## Results

### Primary conditional local-score association and spatial-null analyses

In GSE278687, the primary analysis gave a patient-level median local correlation of 0.418 (bootstrap 95% CI 0.312-0.516; 17/18 positive). The hierarchy-matched block null had 0/999 at-least-as-extreme draws (two-sided add-one Monte Carlo P=0.001). The global-Moran- and mask-Moran-targeted graph sensitivities each had 0/1,000 extreme draws (add-one Monte Carlo P=0.000999). Figure 1 summarizes the primary patient-level effect and complementary spatial randomization sensitivities, and Table 3 records the spatial-inference and scoreability audit. Figure 2 provides fixed-quantile representative spatial displays of the score fields. These randomization results apply to the specified local-field statistic, not to cell proximity, cell abundance, or a discrete tissue entity. In GSE277116, the corresponding sample-level median was 0.414 (95% CI 0.287-0.470; 18/18 positive), and each graph sensitivity had 0/1,000 extreme draws. Figure 4 displays this external sample-level technical replication. The lack of public patient identifiers and package-to-patient linkage means this cohort offers external sample-level technical replication, not independent patient-level clinical validation.

### Conditioning, scale and competitive-program sensitivity

The association remained positive in whole-tissue sensitivity analyses without DC-core residualization (GSE278687 median r=0.399; GSE277116 r=0.383) and with DC-core residualization (0.376 and 0.369). Table 4 summarizes these conditioning and neighbourhood-scale sensitivities. With the adjusted mregDC-like score, masks above the median, mean, and 75th percentile for DC-core gave medians of 0.428, 0.418, and 0.449 in GSE278687 and 0.413, 0.414, and 0.419 in GSE277116 (Supplementary Figure S7 and Table S26). At k=4/6/12, medians were 0.375/0.418/0.475 and 0.372/0.414/0.473, respectively (Supplementary Figure S8 and Table S26). Supplementary Table S27 reports raw neighbour-distance distributions separately because GSE278687 image pixels and GSE277116 array coordinates do not share a micrometre scale. These post-hoc analyses show the same direction across tested definitions; they do not replace the primary endpoint.

After non-overlapping broad-T adjustment, median correlations remained positive (0.331 and 0.350). Figure 3 and Table 2 summarize the competitive-program and joint-model results. Removing `CCL19` reduced the medians to 0.303 and 0.203. Non-Tfh CD4 was at least as strongly associated as the operational Tfh-like program (0.436 and 0.440), which argues against Tfh specificity. In a joint local-field model, the median standardized Tfh coefficients were 0.158 and 0.169, and median Tfh VIF values were 1.84 and 1.53. These quantities are descriptive because the model does not account for spatial residual correlation.

### Independent author-annotated single-cell program-attribution QC

The integrity-checked GSE202051 object contained 224,988 cells, 22,164 genes, 43 patients, and 44 samples with author-provided hierarchical labels. Table 5 summarizes the prespecified single-cell comparison and its interpretive boundary. Level-3 labels included 91 activated DCs from 26 patients, cDC2, cDC1, pDC, macrophage, CD4+ T, Treg, and several CD8 states, but no Tfh category. In the designated primary single-cell comparison, the frozen mregDC-like score was higher in activated DCs than cDC2 across 23 paired patients (median paired difference 0.479, bootstrap 95% CI 0.424-0.668; two-sided P=2.38e-6). Requiring at least 3 or 5 cells in each label per patient retained 9 and 7 paired patients; both exploratory contrasts remained positive (difference 0.718, 95% CI 0.359-0.933, two-sided P=0.00391, FDR q=0.00641; and 0.718, 95% CI 0.359-0.747, two-sided P=0.0156, FDR q=0.0169). Comparisons with cDC1, pDC, macrophage states, and unadjusted scoring were exploratory. `CCL19` was detected in 1/91 activated DCs (1.1%); its removal increased the activated-DC-versus-cDC2 difference to 0.573 (exploratory FDR q=1.40e-5). All leave-one-gene-out contrasts remained positive, although removing `LAMP3` produced the largest attenuation (difference 0.280; exploratory two-sided P=1.53e-4). These deletions test dependence on the predefined score, not molecular contribution or mechanism.

In exploratory comparisons, the operational Tfh-like score was higher in author-labelled CD4+ T cells than in Treg and CD8+ T cells, but the CD4-versus-Treg contrast was small (25 paired patients; median difference 0.061; FDR q=0.00770). Removing `IL7R` reversed the median difference. In CD4+ T cells, `CXCL13`, `PDCD1`, and `BCL6` were detected in 0.22%, 1.31%, and 1.74% of cells, whereas `IL7R` was detected in 72.1%. GSE202051 therefore supports higher fixed mregDC-like scores in activated DCs, but it does not identify Tfh cells or support a Tfh-specific interpretation of the spatial result (Supplementary Figure S9 and Tables S30-S33).

## Discussion

Within the stated score, residualization, mask, and smoothing definitions, both datasets showed the same direction of association between local score fields. Since the non-Tfh CD4 program was at least as strongly associated, the appropriate framing is an mregDC-like and T-cell/lymphoid program association; Tfh-like remains only the fixed operational program. This question differs from recent PDAC spatial studies of neural regions [8], treatment-remodelled TLS [9], and immune dysfunction [10], which address morphology, clinical context, or cell-state mapping. GSE278687 supplies patient-level evidence, while GSE277116 provides technical support across FFPE and frozen samples. The hierarchy-matched block null aggregates null effects at the same patient level as the observed statistic. Global- and mask-targeted graph sensitivities closely match their own outcome-autocorrelation targets but leave mismatch on the alternative scale. Together, the null analyses make the result less dependent on a simple random-outcome explanation. They do not identify a biological entity, cell relationship, or tissue organization.

The estimand is conditional by design. We calculated local correlations only in spots above the within-section mean DC-core score and also residualized the mregDC-like score for DC-core. This targets DC-core-enriched tissue, not the entire tumour. Selecting an immune/myeloid-enriched mask can change the observed association by restricting tissue context, while DC-core adjustment can remove biologically related variation from the mregDC-like score. In post-hoc analyses, the association remained positive in whole tissue without DC-core adjustment (median r=0.399 in GSE278687 and 0.383 in GSE277116), whole tissue with adjustment (0.376 and 0.369), and masks above the median, mean, and 75th percentile (Supplementary Figure S7 and Table S26). These results support stability across those alternatives, but they do not turn the primary estimand into a tumour-wide one.

The controls also show what the analysis cannot resolve. Smoothed spot scores can correlate because both follow an immune-rich context or another unmeasured spatial gradient, a relevant concern in the heterogeneous PDAC microenvironment [31,32]. Broad-T adjustment and the joint model retained a positive Tfh-like component, but the non-Tfh CD4 association was at least as large in both cohorts. The data therefore do not support Tfh-specific language. Median joint-model Tfh coefficients were 0.158 and 0.169, with median VIF values of 1.84 and 1.53; per-section audits are in Supplementary Table S29. These coefficients remain descriptive because the model lacks spatially valid standard errors and does not model residual spatial dependence, so it cannot identify an independent or causal effect. Removing `CCL19` attenuated the association and limits mregDC-specific wording; persistence after deletion does not make `CCL19` irrelevant. The near-zero macrophage comparison is a control result, not evidence that macrophages have no role in PDAC immunity.

The spatial nulls improve on ordinary label permutations, which destroy autocorrelation and can make local-field associations appear overly precise. Figure 5 summarizes the leakage-aware holdout and leave-one-unit sensitivity. Both nulls hold the observed mregDC-like field fixed and change only the Tfh-like outcome, so they test the score-field relationship conditional on that field rather than a joint spatial process or cellular association. The leakage-aware block holdout offers a separate check: its attenuated but positive unit-level summaries suggest that the direction does not depend entirely on reconstructing each local field from the full tissue, but the holdout is not a second inferential P value. Array-block permutations preserve block membership but not all within-block dependence. The graph null preserves ranks and matches one outcome Moran target, but it cannot capture every nonstationary anatomical gradient or unknown tissue process. Local fields using k=4, 6, or 12 neighbours were positive in both cohorts (0.375/0.418/0.475 and 0.372/0.414/0.473). Raw neighbour distances are image pixels in GSE278687 and archive array units in GSE277116, not a shared biological scale (Supplementary Table S26 and Figure S8). A score-defined local association remains compatible with a common spatial context.

Several design features limit generalisation. GSE277116 reflects a neoadjuvant-treatment context, combines FFPE and frozen packages, lacks public patient identifiers, and includes only packages that met archive, QC, and primary-program scoreability criteria. It supports sample-level technical replication but cannot establish patient-level reproducibility, untreated-disease generalisability, treatment specificity, outcome association, or package independence. Scores depend on the frozen gene lists, the within-section DC-core threshold, and platform-specific sparsity. The lists and primary analysis were fixed before the unified rerun but were not prospectively preregistered. Graph-null refinements, `CCL19` deletion, reference-program comparisons, joint models, mask alternatives, neighbourhood sizes, and scoreability thresholds were added as the analysis evolved; their rationale is documented in Supplementary Data S2. They are robustness, interpretive, or descriptive analyses, not independent confirmatory tests. The high zero-score fraction of the primary programs in GSE277116 and the sparsity of some competitive programs also limit cell-abundance interpretation.

The single-cell analyses provide attribution QC rather than validation of the spatial association. GSE217845 has independent human PDAC counts but no accessible author labels. Because its marker gates partly define the scored programs, enrichment in gated cells would be circular; we use it only to document gene detectability and gate feasibility. Ambient RNA, doublets, and transitional states remain possible. GSE202051 preserves author-traceable labels and supports higher fixed mregDC-like scores in activated DCs. The primary comparison is patient paired, and the low number of activated DCs was tested with >=3 and >=5 cells-per-label sensitivities, which retained 9 and 7 patients; both are exploratory. The reference lacks an author-defined Tfh category, and the Tfh-like score is dominated by broad T-cell components with `IL7R` dependence. This sharpens the non-specificity boundary but does not remove it. Neither single-cell analysis tests spatial association or cell interaction. A stronger follow-up would preregister the programs, local statistic, and spatial null; use an independent, preferably untreated resection cohort with patient identifiers and linked pathology; define TLS or immune aggregates histologically; and combine annotated single-cell profiling with multiplex imaging of LAMP3/CCR7, dendritic-lineage markers, CD4/CXCR5/PD-1/ICOS, and non-overlapping lineage markers. TLS reviews support assessing molecular, cellular, and structural features together [33,34]. Evidence from another tumour setting shows that mregDCs can occur in a spatial immune context [35], but it cannot be treated as a PDAC mechanism. These experiments could assign cell states and morphology, but would still not prove recruitment or causality.

The defensible claim is narrower: locally smoothed scores show a directionally concordant association under the stated score, mask, residualization, and smoothing definitions. The result does not infer TLS morphology or maturation, mregDC or Tfh abundance, a cellular niche, recruitment, causal communication, tissue organization, prognosis, or therapeutic response. It is a testable signal for pathology-linked and functional follow-up.

## Conclusions

In public PDAC spatial datasets, mregDC-like and T-cell/lymphoid programs showed a directionally concordant association between locally smoothed scores under the stated score, residualization, DC-core mask, and neighbourhood definitions. GSE278687 provides patient-level evidence, and GSE277116 provides sample-level technical replication. Spatial nulls, conditioning and scale sensitivities, non-overlapping references, joint-model diagnostics, `CCL19` sensitivity, scoreability audits, and leakage-aware holdout analyses define the limits of this result. The competitive analyses do not support a Tfh-specific interpretation. This is a score-defined spatial association, not evidence of a cellular niche, TLS state, tissue organization, or cell interaction.
